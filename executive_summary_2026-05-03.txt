PROJECT OVERVIEW
----------------
SenseEase is an NPU-powered visual comfort system for HP AI PCs that performs
real-time optical-flow interception at the rendering layer to reduce two major
display-related risks:

  - Visually Induced Motion Sickness (VIMS): sensory conflict between visual
    motion and vestibular stillness.

  - Digital Eye Strain (DES): cumulative strain from high-contrast transitions,
    luminance instability, and prolonged flicker exposure.

Product intent: provide clinically meaningful comfort improvement without
requiring user behavior change, while preserving real-time interaction quality.


FOUR-LAYER ENGINEERING ARCHITECTURE
-------------------------------------
  System Core Philosophy:
    SenseEase is designed as an OS-level integrated visual assistance layer
    for HP AI PCs, shifting mitigation from reactive comfort recovery to
    high-frequency preventive intervention before symptoms escalate.

  Layer 1 - Sensing & Telemetry:
    Hardware decode telemetry, DOM scroll velocity, mouse/keyboard streams,
    and ambient-light signals are fused into local Optical Flow Intensity
    (OFI) estimation. HP Look to Move gaze vectors and cursor coordinates act
    as intent proxy for foveal-zone localization. All core flow computation is
    local-first and camera-optional, minimizing privacy and compute overhead.

  Layer 2 - Inference & Pressure Computation:
    NPU executes just-in-time pressure inference to maintain a rolling VIMS
    Pressure Index. Efference Copy logic separates Driver Mode (active input)
    from Passenger Mode (passive viewing), reducing active-input influence to
    near-zero/low-weight when user intent is confirmed. A leaky integrator
    with proportional attenuation is used to suppress mask on/off ping-pong:
    when masking is active, effective flow is attenuated (e.g., ~20%) with a
    small recovery bias to keep pressure evolution visually and numerically
    stable around release thresholds.

  Layer 3 - Rendering & Dynamic Intervention:
    In real-world product form, SenseEase is positioned for Windows compositing
    interception (DWM path) with sub-150ms response targets. In the current
    demo build, WebGL + controller pipelines implement two intervention classes:
    1) Extreme VIMS paths (Scenarios 1/4): absolute peripheral occlusion plus
       Chang-Hain Stationary Anchor (screen-locked white grid) for spatial
       reference stabilization.
    2) High-frequency DES paths: preserve real-time update continuity while
       applying gaze-contingent peripheral suppression, dynamic blur, and
       temporal smoothing to non-foveal high-stimulus regions.

  Layer 4 - Feedback & Progressive Habituation:
    HP IQ serves as the AI orchestration interface for natural-language
    discomfort feedback and long-cycle adaptation control. Complaint-triggered
    reset returns protection toward the user's current comfort zone, while
    Progressive Habituation expands clear-FOV exposure in micro-steps during
    symptom-silent periods (target behavior: ~1%/sec after sustained silence,
    e.g., 120s) under the Chang-Hain rehabilitation framework.

    - Real-time telemetry surfaced: NPU Load, Optical Flow, VIMS Pressure,

    - Demo target routing corrected so OFI/VIMS detection stays active across
      visible scenarios.
    - Video lifecycle synchronization fixed (play/playing/pause/ended) to keep
      pressure/flow updates consistent under NPU ON/OFF transitions.
    - Adaptive intensity path hardened with:
      1) filtered target intensity,
      2) per-frame delta clamping,
      3) fade hysteresis (stable fade-in/fade-out).
    - Shader stability pass completed: deterministic peripheral opacity,
      reduced shimmer, preserved Chang-Hain anchor behavior.

  Scenario Packaging Decision:
    - Scenario 2 code is retained for future expansion.
    - Public demo flow is currently presented as Scenarios 1-4 for clearer
      narrative and stronger end-to-end reliability in judging sessions.
