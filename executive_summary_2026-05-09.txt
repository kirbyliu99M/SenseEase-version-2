GRAND STRATEGY CHANGE
---------------------
The single most important shift since the prior summary: SenseEase is no longer
a five-scenario tab demo. The product surface has been collapsed into ONE
unified Main Scenario that runs three protection layers concurrently on a
shared video substrate.

  Before (v1, May 2):                    After (v2, May 9):
  ─────────────────────                  ─────────────────────────────
  S1  Extreme VIMS                       Main Scenario
  S2  Trading Terminal      ──────►        ├── Chang-Hain VIMS protection
  S3  Adaptive Lighting                    ├── Progressive Habituation
  S4  Prolonged Office                     └── Circadian DES protection
  S5  Habituation                        (S1–S5 panels are hidden husks
                                          retained only for fallback)

Why this matters for the demo. Five isolated panels forced the presenter to
navigate between tabs and explain each scenario in sequence — diluting the
clinical story and burning booth time. The unified Main Scenario lets a
visitor watch a single piece of motion content while the system simultaneously:
  - intercepts visually-induced motion sickness via the Chang-Hain field-of-
    view mask (white stationary anchor grid + radial smoothstep transition);
  - expands the clear central FOV in 2-second adaptation cycles, training
    vestibular tolerance;
  - shifts the screen colour temperature in response to ambient light to
    suppress digital eye strain.

Two further strategic shifts ride alongside the unification:

  1. Camera goes from forbidden to first-class optional. The original "no
     camera required — zero privacy risk" line has been retired in favour of
     "camera optional; all gaze and lux processing on-device, no frame data
     leaves the machine." This unlocks the gaze-contingent demo path that
     makes the Chang-Hain FOV mask actually responsive to where the user is
     looking, and the lux-driven circadian path that makes the DES story
     real instead of simulated.

  2. OpenVINO becomes the marquee acceleration layer. Gaze inference can run
     either in-browser (MediaPipe FaceLandmarker, the privacy-first default)
     or via a local Python WebSocket bridge that compiles OpenVINO models on
     the Intel NPU / iGPU / CPU. The bridge is the visible AI PC story — the
     UI surfaces the live device with a colour-coded pill ("Intel NPU
     3.4 ms") so the audience can see the NPU doing real work.

The Chang-Hain framework remains the clinical core and was not touched. The
v2 architectural changes layer ON TOP of the V12 shader and the V14
stabilisations — none of the prior visual comfort work was discarded.


PROJECT OVERVIEW
----------------
SenseEase is an NPU-powered visual comfort system for HP AI PCs. It targets
two related conditions:

  - Visually Induced Motion Sickness (VIMS): ~14.8% of display users; sensory
    conflict between the visual cortex (motion detected) and the vestibular
    system (no motion registered) producing nausea.

  - Digital Eye Strain (DES): ~80% of office workers; unmanaged luminance
    transitions, high-contrast window switches, and prolonged blue-light
    exposure.

The reach figure is 97.6% of display users with some form of visual
discomfort, with a clinical sub-path for the 2.8% with diagnosed vestibular
disorders.


FOUR-LAYER ENGINEERING ARCHITECTURE
------------------------------------
Layer 1 — Sensing:
  HP Look to Move gaze vectors, mouse/keyboard/touchpad event streams, and
  DOM scroll velocity fuse into Optical Flow Intensity (OFI). NEW in v2: an
  optional on-device webcam pipeline driven by either Google MediaPipe
  FaceLandmarker (in-browser WASM) or the OpenVINO Bridge (local Python
  service). NEW in v2: ambient lux comes from the AmbientLightSensor API
  when available, otherwise from 32×32 webcam-frame luminance sampling.

Layer 2 — Inference:
  ALSTM-FCN-style rolling VIMS Pressure Index (leaky integrator with a
  noise gate at OFI 18 and an active-intent dampener of 10). Efference
  Copy weighting at 0.2× in Driver Mode (user-initiated) and 1.5× in
  Passenger Mode (passive scrolling, video).

Layer 3 — Rendering:
  WebGL DitheringShader with the Chang-Hain (CH) Three-Zone Framework:
    - Zone 1 (r < r_in)     : fully transparent foveal vision
    - Zone 2 (r_in → r_out) : smooth dark gradient, no grid (anti-flow)
    - Zone 3 (r > r_out)    : dark mask + WHITE stationary anchor grid
                              (Stabilising effect — vestibular ground-truth)
  The shader was hardened through five GPU-driver iterations (mod-PI rand()
  precision, farthest-corner normalisation, canvas-rect resize, smoothstep
  AA at 1.2px width, dither boundary stabilisation) and is unchanged in v2.

Layer 4 — Feedback:
  HP IQ Chatbot collects complaints. Habituation Scheduler relaxes the
  mask at 1%/sec after 120 seconds of silent comfort, modelling adaptive
  brain plasticity. NEW in v2: the Habituation Engine has been re-targeted
  onto the unified Main Scenario video so it is no longer a standalone tab.

  - EyeTracker.js evolved from a mouse-position stub into a full filtering

Plan D — OpenVINO Bridge upgrade (committed locally, awaiting push):
  - Server now defaults to --device AUTO with a NPU → GPU → CPU compile
    chain. The actual device selected at compile time is exposed via
    backend.actual_device.
  - Hello handshake on every WebSocket connect:
      { type: "hello", protocol: 1, backend: "openvino"|"opencv",
        device: "NPU"|"GPU"|"CPU"|"OpenCV-CPU", face_model, headpose_model }
  - Per-frame gaze responses include rolling inferenceMs (EMA over the
    real model time, not the wire-trip time).
  - OpenVinoBridgeTracker.js parses hello, EMA-tracks inferenceMs, exposes
    getBackendLabel() and getDiagnostics().remote / .inferenceMs. Static
    OpenVinoBridgeTracker.probe() lets the UI cheaply check availability
    without committing to a full webcam stream.
  - Auto-probe at page load + every 12 s while visible + click-to-retry
    hint so users see when the bridge comes online without page refresh.
  - Visible backend status pill in the Main Scenario header. Colour-coded
    by device:
      green   = Intel NPU
      cyan    = Intel GPU (iGPU)
      amber   = Intel CPU (OpenVINO)
      orange  = OpenCV (no AI accel)
      blue    = MediaPipe (in-browser)
      red     = bridge requested but unreachable (fallback active)
    Live latency chip ("· 3.4 ms") trails the device label and updates at
    2 Hz off OpenVinoBridgeTracker.getDiagnostics().inferenceMs.


CURRENTLY FACED ISSUES + RESOLUTIONS
-------------------------------------
The path from v1 to v2 surfaced five concrete issues. All are resolved or
mitigated; the post-mortem is recorded here so the team has institutional
memory.

Issue 1 — Bridge claimed OpenVINO acceleration that wasn't running.
  Symptom:  UI button read "Backend: OpenVINO Bridge", but no process
            listened on port 8765, the runtime silently fell back to
            MediaPipe, and the demo audience had no way to tell.
  Root cause: three-way mismatch.
            (a) Bridge server wasn't started.
            (b) Even when started without --face-model flags, the server
                ran the OpenCV Haar cascade fallback — the UI label still
                read "OpenVINO Bridge".
            (c) Server was hardcoded to compile_model(model, "CPU"), so
                even with OpenVINO models loaded the NPU never lit up.
  Resolution (Plan D):
            - Server: --device AUTO with NPU → GPU → CPU compile chain.
            - Server: hello handshake announces the actual_device the
              models compiled for, plus the backend type ("openvino" vs
              "opencv").
            - Client: OpenVinoBridgeTracker parses hello and exposes the
              precise label.
            - UI: a colour-coded backend pill shows the live device. Demo
              audiences now see "Intel NPU 3.4 ms" (or
              "OpenCV (no AI accel)") — not just an opaque toggle.

Issue 2 — Gaze tracking went silent for 66 seconds of demo time.
  Symptom:  Gaze Debug panel showed mode: lost, mapping: fallback,
            calibration: none, stale: 66783 ms — but MediaPipe was clearly
            still alive (fps EMA 50.7). Webcam mode was enabled, but the
            FOV mask never reacted to the user's gaze.
  Root cause: MediaPipe stopped detecting the face. The tiny 80×60 webcam
            preview made it impossible to notice the user had drifted out
            of frame, and minFaceDetectionConfidence: 0.65 rejected too
            aggressively under typical booth lighting.
  Resolution:
            - Confidence thresholds dropped 0.65 → 0.45 across detection,
              presence, and tracking gates in GazeTracker._ensureMediaPipe().
            - Webcam preview replaced with a 200×150 self-view tile in a
              dedicated "main-webcam-tray" with explicit position-check
              copy ("centre your face, ~50 cm, even lighting").
            - Live gaze-lost watcher at 4 Hz reads
              eyeTracker.getDiagnostics().staleMs and surfaces a red
              banner the moment samples stop, with a direct CTA to
              "Recalibrate Gaze". Auto-clears when samples resume.

Issue 3 — Circadian DES protection felt fake.
  Symptom:  S4 was a 1-hour office time-lapse with a scripted overlay.
            It always animated cool → warm regardless of the real room
            light. Booth visitors who turned the room lights up or down
            saw no response.
  Root cause: there was no environmental sensor wired in.
  Resolution:
            - LuxSensor.js prefers the AmbientLightSensor API where
              supported (Chrome desktop with the generic-sensor flag, or
              Android Chrome on devices with the hardware sensor).
            - Webcam-frame luminance sampling at 2 Hz as the universal
              fallback. 32×32 downscale, Rec.709 luminance, mapped to
              warmth ∈ [0,1] (dim room → deep warm, bright room → cool).
            - Demo presenters can now darken or brighten the booth area
              and the screen colour shifts in real time. Stats panel's
              DES chart and the new "Warm Shift Time" KPI track the
              cumulative protection.

Issue 4 — Five-scenario tab structure diluted the demo.
  Symptom:  Booth visitors had to commit to a tab choice before seeing
            anything compelling, and the strongest visual story (Chang-
            Hain mask + Habituation + circadian) was split across three
            disconnected panels.
  Root cause: the original v1 architecture was scenario-first, optimised
            for breadth over depth.
  Resolution: see GRAND STRATEGY CHANGE above. Sub-tabs hidden, panels
            retained as DOM husks for engine compatibility, single Main
            Scenario panel composes the full story on one video.

Issue 5 — Privacy claim conflicted with the strongest demo path.
  Symptom:  v1 documentation explicitly said "no camera required — zero
            privacy risk", which precluded the gaze-contingent and
            ambient-light demo paths that v2 needed.
  Root cause: the privacy promise was over-broad. Camera-on-device
            processing is a different privacy posture from camera-off.
  Resolution:
            - executive_summary_2026-05-02.txt Layer 1 paragraph rewritten
              to "Camera optional; gaze and ambient-lux processing run
              fully on-device via Google MediaPipe FaceLandmarker and the
              AmbientLightSensor API, so no frame data ever leaves the
              machine."
            - index.html Layer 1 architecture card matches.
            - Main Scenario panel carries an in-context privacy footnote
              next to the Webcam Mode toggle.
            - Default mode remains mouse + simulated circadian, so the
              no-camera fallback story is intact for visitors who decline.


DEMO FLOW (BOOTH SCRIPT)
------------------------
1. Page loads. Auto-probe pings the OpenVINO bridge in the background. The
   status strip shows either:
     "✓ OpenVINO Bridge ready on Intel NPU. Click Backend: OpenVINO Bridge
      to switch."
   or:
     "OpenVINO Bridge offline — using in-browser MediaPipe. Run
      tools/openvino_bridge_server.py for Intel NPU acceleration."

2. Click Enable Webcam Mode. One getUserMedia prompt; preview tile
   activates. Calibration runs once (centre-lock baseline ~3 s, dot in
   the middle of the screen).

3. Optional: click Backend: OpenVINO Bridge if the bridge is up. The
   backend pill flips green and reads "● Intel NPU · 3.4 ms".

4. Click Start Neural Adaptation (or hit play on the video). Three
   protection layers engage simultaneously:
     - The Chang-Hain mask follows your gaze; the white anchor grid is
       locked to screen coords (vestibular ground-truth).
     - The clear FOV expands every two seconds (Habituation engine).
     - The circadian overlay shifts as the booth's ambient light changes
       (LuxSensor-driven warmth value into setCircadianWarmth).

5. If the visitor reports nausea via "I feel dizzy" in HP IQ Feedback,
   the Habituation Adaptation Reset fires: FOV shrinks 15%, an 8-second
   cooldown locks the engine, and the event log records the reset.

6. Switch to Statistics tab. Three live charts (VIMS Pressure with
   protection, baseline pressure without, DES protection over time) and
   five KPI tiles (FOV Intercepts, Peak Optical Flow, Peak VIMS Pressure,
   Warm Shift Time, Session Uptime) reflect what just happened.

KNOWN GAPS
----------
- public/vendor/mediapipe/models/face_landmarker.task is shipped offline so
  the demo runs without internet, but the file is not yet listed as a CI
  artefact — fresh clones must download it once or run `npm run build`
  with internet to populate.
- Bridge server requires Python 3.10+ and the OpenVINO 2024 runtime to
  enable the NPU path. The OpenCV fallback works without OpenVINO but the
  status pill correctly labels it "OpenCV (no AI accel)".
- AmbientLightSensor API support is uneven (Chrome desktop behind a flag,
  some Android only). Webcam luminance fallback handles every other case.
- ~1.5 s WebSocket connection timeout on the bridge probe — fast enough
  to feel responsive, but a slow Python startup on cold boot can outrun it.
  Not a correctness issue (auto-retry every 12 s); cosmetic only.
